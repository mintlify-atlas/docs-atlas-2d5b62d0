Ghost's native search works well for site-wide queries, but it has two notable limitations: it cannot scope results to a specific tag, it does not search page content (only posts), and it does not index post body text. **Child Search** fills all three gaps — providing a focused, full-text search experience that is automatically injected wherever readers are most likely to need it.

---

## Where Child Search Auto-Injects

Child Search activates automatically on three types of pages — no configuration required:

- **Individual Tag pages** — readers browsing a specific tag can search within that tag's posts without leaving the page.
- **Individual Post pages — Related Posts section** — a search bar appears in the Related Posts area, scoped to the primary tag of the post being read.
- **Pages using a grid-layout template** — curated resource pages and tool directories get an instant search overlay powered by the same engine.

---

## How FuseJS Powers the Search

Child Search is built on **FuseJS**, a lightweight fuzzy-search library. Its search priority order ensures the most relevant results surface first:

1. **Title** — the highest-weight signal; matches here rank first.
2. **Description** — excerpt and meta description fields contribute to relevance scoring.
3. **Body text** — the full post content is indexed, enabling deep-content discovery that Ghost's native search cannot perform.

The search results display the **top 5 best-matching posts** for any given query.

<Note>
  Indexing body text is the key difference from Ghost's built-in search. Where native search matches only titles and descriptions, Child Search finds matches anywhere inside the post content — making it significantly more powerful for content-heavy sites.
</Note>

---

## Behavior by Context

### Tag Pages

On an individual tag page, Child Search functions as a familiar, native-feeling search experience scoped entirely to that tag's content. The search bar is prominently placed to be immediately interactive for visitors — and because it indexes body text, readers can search for a concept mentioned deep within an article, not just in its headline.

### Related Posts Section

When Child Search appears in the Related Posts section of an individual post, it automatically performs a search scoped to **all posts within the primary tag** of that post. This keeps every suggestion contextually relevant: a reader on a JavaScript tutorial will only see JavaScript-tagged posts in both the recommended list and any search results they trigger.

---

## Use Cases

- **Course or resource hubs:** Readers can instantly find a specific lesson or tool without scrolling through a long tag archive.
- **Multilingual sites:** Scoping search to a tag means language-specific content stays isolated from unrelated results.
- **Affiliate or product pages:** Grid-layout pages with many product entries become instantly filterable without any custom code.
- **Long-form tutorial series:** Readers can jump directly to the post that answers their specific question, even if the keyword only appears in the body text.
